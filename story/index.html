Rwanda's Road to Formula 1

From a history the world must remember to a new ambition on the global stage, Rwanda prepares for a possible new chapter in motorsport.

StoryCast Editorial October 19, 2026 News feature · 6-minute read

A milestone announced

On October 13, 2026, Formula 1 announced a ten-year agreement to bring the Formula 1 African Grand Prix to Rwanda. The championship is expected to return to Africa in the early 2030s, once a new purpose-built circuit near Bugesera has been completed.

The planned venue is around 40 kilometres south of Kigali, close to the new Kigali International Airport. The circuit is intended to use the area's natural hills and landscape to create a distinctive racing experience.

This is an agreement for a future event, not a race that has already taken place. The inaugural race date and further development details are still to be announced.

Remembering the past

Rwanda's story cannot be told honestly without acknowledging the 1994 Genocide against the Tutsi. More than a historical reference, it represents the lives lost, families changed forever, and communities that continue to remember and honour those who died.

A story about a sporting milestone should not suggest that a new agreement erases this history or the grief that remains. Instead, it can recognise that Rwanda's past and its ambitions for the future exist alongside one another.

Remembering matters. It gives context to the country being presented to the world and honours the people whose lives are part of its history.

What could this mean for Rwanda?

Hosting a Formula 1 race could bring international attention, visitors, investment and new business opportunities. The project may also create demand for skills in engineering, technology, hospitality, logistics, media and event management.

For young Rwandans, the opportunity could reach beyond watching cars race. It could inspire interest in motorsport engineering, software, data analysis, vehicle technology and careers connected to major international events.

However, these outcomes are not automatic. The long-term value of the project will depend on how it is delivered, how much local talent and businesses participate, and whether the benefits are shared widely.

The circuit is still planned, and the race is expected in the early 2030s. The real measure of success will emerge over time.
